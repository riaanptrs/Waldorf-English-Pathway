# Grade 7 Course Map

## Course purpose

A self-paced English course for Brazilian learners aged about 12–13. It adapts Grade 7 Waldorf language-and-literature work for EFL learners. The programme moves from close observation to imaginative writing, poetry, story structure, and supported literary analysis.

Each lesson offers Guided, Core, and Stretch paths. The subject stays shared; only the amount of language support changes.

## Unit 0 — Start Here

**Outcome:** Learner profile, support level, first short piece of writing.

| Lesson | Title | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | Welcome to the Pathway | How lessons, drafts, audio, glossary, and reflection work |
| 0.2 | Find Your Starting Point | Placement check: reading, vocabulary, writing, confidence |
| 0.3 | My English Journey | Short learner profile and learning goal |

## Unit 1 — Observe & Describe

**Outcome:** A precise 120–180 word description of a natural object.

| Lesson | Title | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Look Closely | Choose and observe a leaf, stone, feather, shell, or pine cone |
| 1.2 | Exact Words | Specific nouns, accurate colours, size, shape, and texture |
| 1.3 | Show, Don’t Just Tell | Sensory detail and word pictures |
| 1.4 | Strong Sentences | Sentence combining; rhythm and variety |
| 1.5 | Writing Studio: Natural Object | Draft, revise, self-check, portfolio entry |

## Unit 2 — Wishes, Choices & Voice

**Outcome:** An 80–120 word supported personal-response paragraph.

| Lesson | Title | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | A Wish for the Future | Personal voice and future language |
| 2.2 | Reasons that Connect | First, also, because, for example, in the end |
| 2.3 | Choices and Consequences | Explain an opinion respectfully |
| 2.4 | A Natural Wonder | Read Wordsworth's *My Heart Leaps Up*; imagery and wonder |
| 2.5 | Writing Studio: Wonder Response | Describe a source of wonder and why it matters |

## Unit 3 — Poetry & Word Pictures

**Outcome:** A short illustrated poetry collection.

| Lesson | Title | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Images in Three Lines | Haiku: sensory image before perfect syllable counting |
| 3.2 | A Word Becomes a Poem | Acrostic and cinquain |
| 3.3 | Contrast in a Diamond | Diamante: word classes and opposites |
| 3.4 | Play with Sound | Limerick as an optional rhyme challenge |
| 3.5 | Animal Poem | Free verse or rhyming poem |
| 3.6 | Poetry from Songs | Simile, metaphor, and content-safe lyric excerpts |
| 3.7 | Poetry Portfolio | Select, revise, illustrate, and record one poem |

## Unit 4 — Stories with Shape

**Outcome:** A coherent nine-sentence story, revised into a 250–400 word narrative.

| Lesson | Title | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | A Beginning that Opens a Door | Character and setting |
| 4.2 | Paint the Place | Specific setting details |
| 4.3 | A Companion Appears | Animal or meaningful companion |
| 4.4 | The Problem | Obstacle and rising tension |
| 4.5 | The Moment that Changes Everything | Climax and vivid verbs |
| 4.6 | Bring the Story Home | Resolution and meaningful ending |
| 4.7 | Writing Studio: Story with Structure | Draft, revise, portfolio |

## Unit 5 — Read, Think & Respond

**Outcome:** A supported three-paragraph literary response; Stretch learners prepare a five-paragraph essay.

| Lesson | Title | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | A Poem Can Mean More than One Thing | *The Road Not Taken*: vocabulary, ambiguity, evidence |
| 5.2 | Show Not Tell in Poetry | *Cargoes*: lists, sound, contrast, imagery |
| 5.3 | One Metaphor Can Carry a Story | *O Captain! My Captain!*: extended metaphor and historical context |
| 5.4 | The Night as a Picture | *The Highwayman*: imagery, simile, metaphor, and narrative tension |
| 5.5 | Read Like a Writer | Character, setting, conflict, climax, and resolution in an adapted extract |
| 5.6 | From Answer to Paragraph | Claim, evidence, explanation |
| 5.7 | From Paragraph to Essay | Three-paragraph response; optional five-paragraph structure |
| 5.8 | Final Portfolio Reflection | Select work, reflect on growth, set the next goal |

## Assessment and progress

Progress is demonstrated through work, not a single test:

- completion of practices and reflections;
- revision of draft writing;
- unit portfolio pieces;
- final literary response;
- learner self-assessment.

The site should save completion state and drafts per lesson. It should not automatically score creative writing as right or wrong.

## Content and copyright notes

- *My Heart Leaps Up*, *The Road Not Taken*, *Cargoes*, *O Captain! My Captain!*, and *The Highwayman* must be used only after checking the text's public-domain status for the intended audience and jurisdiction.
- Contemporary songs should use teacher-approved short excerpts or link to lawful sources; do not reproduce full lyrics.
- All explanations, exercises, model answers, and EFL scaffolding will be original course material.
