Unit 1 · Lesson 2

Exact Words

Good writers choose words that help a reader see the real object—not just a general idea.

40–45 minutesGuided A2 · Core B1Portfolio: observation notes

IN THIS LESSON

1. Notice2. Learn words3. Put adjectives in order4. Choose5. Build sentences6. Write7. Check

1. Notice · 4 min

From vague to vivid.

Read the two descriptions. Which one helps you picture the object?

Too general

I found a nice thing. It is brown. It has pieces.

More exact

I found a small brown pine cone. It has dry, overlapping scales with pointed edges.

The second description is better because it names the object and gives precise details.

Português: Uma palavra exata ajuda o leitor a imaginar o objeto. Em vez de thing, diga o nome do objeto: leaf, stone, shell, pine cone.

3. Learn · 6 min

Put describing words in a helpful order.

In English, we usually put describing words before the noun. When you use more than one adjective, their order helps the sentence sound natural.

A useful pattern for today

size → shape → colour → noun

small round grey stone

Start with only two or three describing words. You do not need to memorise a long grammar rule today.

Português: Em inglês, os adjetivos normalmente vêm antes do substantivo. Para esta lição, use esta ordem: tamanho → forma → cor → substantivo. Exemplo: a small round grey stone = uma pedra pequena, redonda e cinza.
Not natural

a brown small pine cone

More natural

a small brown pine cone

Why? small tells us the size, and brown tells us the colour. In this pattern, size comes before colour.

Choose the phrase that sounds most natural.

Now choose: dimension before colour.

Ready? In the next activity, build a sentence with narrow + dark + stem. Remember: dimension first, colour second.

6. Write · 10–12 min

Upgrade your object notes.

Use the object from Lesson 1, or choose a new natural object. Write 4–6 sentences. Try to use at least one exact noun, two descriptive words, and one observation verb.

Model: a leaf

This pale green leaf measures about seven centimetres from stem to tip. Thin veins spread across its surface. Its edge curves gently between five pointed tips. The delicate leaf resembles a small open hand.
