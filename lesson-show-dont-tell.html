Unit 1 · Lesson 3

Show, Don’t Just Tell

Help your reader see, feel, and imagine the object through your details.

40–45 minutesGuided A2 · Core B1Portfolio: word-picture notes

IN THIS LESSON

1. Notice2. Learn words3. Choose4. Build sentences5. Write6. Check

1. Notice · 5 min

Let the reader discover it.

Both sentences tell us about a leaf. Which one gives you a clearer picture?

Just tells

The leaf is beautiful.

Shows with details

Thin veins spread across the pale green leaf, and its five pointed tips tremble in the wind.

The second sentence does not use the word beautiful. Instead, it gives details that let the reader make a picture.

Português: Show, don’t just tell = mostre em vez de apenas dizer. Dê detalhes que ajudam o leitor a ver, sentir ou imaginar.

5. Write · 10–12 min

Turn a plain description into a picture.

Choose your object from Lessons 1–2. Write 4–6 sentences. Do not write “It is beautiful” or “It is nice.” Instead, show why it is interesting.

Model: a pine cone

A small pine cone rests in my hand. Its dry scales overlap like a tiny wooden roof. Each pointed edge presses against my fingers. When I shake it, it makes a crisp, quiet sound. It reminds me of a little tower from a forest story.

Use these frames if you need them:
The ______ glistens / bends / trembles ______. · It feels ______ under my fingers. · It makes a ______ sound. · It looks like / reminds me of ______.
