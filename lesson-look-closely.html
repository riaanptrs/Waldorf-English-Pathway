Unit 1 · Lesson 1

Look Closely

A good writer does not only say what something is. A good writer helps us see it.

35 minutesA2–B1Portfolio: Natural object description

IN THIS LESSON

1. Opening2. Notice3. Learn4. Practise5. Create6. Reflect

1. Opening · 3 min

Choose a small natural object.

Find a leaf, stone, feather, shell, pine cone, seed, or small piece of bark. Put it in front of you. You do not need a “beautiful” object—only something real.

Português: Escolha um objeto pequeno da natureza. Olhe para ele com calma, como se fosse a primeira vez.

2. Notice · 8 min

Observe before you write.

Look at your object silently for one minute. Then answer with words, not full sentences. There are no perfect answers.

3. Learn · 7 min

Move from bland to exact.

Too general

It is a brown leaf. It is small.

More exact

It is a curled chestnut leaf, dark burnt sienna at its centre and gold at its dry edges. It is no longer than my thumb.

Exact writing often uses a specific noun, a precise colour, a comparison, and one true detail about shape or texture.

Useful words / Palavras úteis

rough = áspero · smooth = liso · vein = nervura · edge = borda · curled = enrolado · speckled = salpicado · observe = observar com atenção

4. Practise · 7 min

Choose the stronger sentence.

Which sentence helps you see the stone more clearly?

5. Create · 8–10 min

Write your first word picture.

Write 3–5 sentences about your object. Use at least three details from your notes. Do not worry about making it perfect yet.

Sentence starter, if you need it:
In my hand, I hold… It is… Its surface feels… It reminds me of…
0 words
